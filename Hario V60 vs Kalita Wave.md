# Hario V60 vs Kalita Wave

*Not a recipe — an explanation.* Why the physical shape of a pour-over dripper changes how the [[Coffee|coffee]] tastes, even with the exact same beans. The key variable is **water speed**: fast flow makes a bright cup, slow flow makes a heavy one.

## [[Hario V60]] — fast flow, bright cup

- A steep cone with **one large hole** at the bottom.
- Almost zero physical resistance: the water is in continuous free-fall through the grounds.
- Because the water moves so fast, it pulls out mostly the brightest, most acidic notes.
- **Result:** a crisp, light-bodied coffee with high acidity and incredible flavour clarity.

## [[Kalita Wave]] — slow flow, sweet cup

- A **flat bottom** with **three tiny holes**.
- The holes act as a physical speed limit on the water, so it dwells with the coffee grounds much longer.
- That extra contact time extracts deeper sugars.
- **Result:** a sweeter, heavier-bodied cup.

## Summary

|              | Hario V60                                 | Kalita Wave                      |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Shape        | Steep cone, one large hole                | Flat bottom, three small holes   |
| Flow         | Fast, little resistance                   | Slow, restricted                 |
| Contact time | Short                                     | Long                             |
| Cup          | High acidity, light body, flavour clarity | Deep sugars, sweeter, heavy body |

## Technique

The shape also dictates technique. The open V60 demands precise pouring to manually control the speed, since the dripper itself does not slow the water down.
